# About this framework

*Context, assumptions, and positioning for the Foundational Data Product™ Progressive Certification framework.*

---

## Origin

This framework was developed by [Anna Jibgashvili](https://www.linkedin.com/in/annajibgashvili/) as the certification method of the Foundational Data Products™ methodology. It grew out of a repeated experience inside large regulated institutions: the first time an enterprise builds data products where it never had them, it discovers that its source data carries quality issues, broken lineage and unclear ownership. A single pass-or-fail certification gate cannot work in that reality. Almost nothing passes, and the work stalls.

Progressive certification lets the work continue while trust is earned in the open.

## What this framework is for

- Measuring the trust a data product has earned, dimension by dimension, as a percentage that improves over time
- Making that trust visible to every consumer, including AI agents, through the product's passport
- Letting each consumer decide fitness for its own purpose, below Gold or at Gold
- Showing product teams exactly which criteria to advance next
- Giving risk and compliance leaders a dated record of how trustworthy the data was when it was used

## What this framework is not for

- **Not a gate that stops work.** A product below Gold stays registered and usable where its profile fits.
- **Not a one-time audit.** Certification is a current reading, refreshed as data, systems and obligations change.
- **Not a tool selection.** Any platform can implement the measurements.
- **Not a ranking between products or between enterprises.** It reads each product against its own evidence.

## Assumptions the framework makes

**Assumption 1: Evidence over intent.** Scores reflect what is in place today. A control that is planned scores as absent.

**Assumption 2: Owners exist.** Certification depends on named owners who can sign off and who own remediation. Where no owner can be named, that is the first finding.

**Assumption 3: Source teams own source fixes.** Product teams surface source data issues and report on them; the teams that own the source systems own the remediation. The certification shows the gap until it closes.

**Assumption 4: The candidate already qualifies.** Certification applies to products that are enterprise-critical and enterprise-reusable. Whether a candidate qualifies is decided before certification begins.

## Positioning relative to data quality and catalog tools

Data quality tools measure quality. Catalogs record metadata and lineage. Access platforms enforce security. Each answers one question well.

This framework combines them into one trust signal per product, weighted by what trust requires, and attaches that signal to the product itself so it travels wherever the data goes. It also adds what tools alone do not decide: whether the product's meaning agrees with the rest of the enterprise, who has signed off, and whether the enterprise holds its definitions in a form it owns.

## The intellectual basis

**1. Trust is a signal, not a gate.** Binary certification is accurate on the day it is issued and uncertain every day after. A continuous, weighted signal stays current and shows where the gaps are.

**2. AI consumes whatever it is given.** An AI system does not refuse uncertified data; it operates on it confidently. The trust profile has to reach the agent at the moment of use, in a form it can read.

**3. Averages need floors.** A weighted average lets strength in one dimension hide weakness in another. Some weaknesses, such as unprotected data or untraceable origin, cannot be absorbed by strength elsewhere.

For deeper development of these arguments, see [Trust Is Not a Gate. It Is a Signal That Must Travel.](https://annajibgashvili.substack.com/p/coming-soon) on Substack.
